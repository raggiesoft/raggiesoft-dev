Narrative Origin: From /books/aubrie Plot Device to Real Language

Most programming languages start in a corporate compiler lab or a university thesis committee. Fin# (Financial Sharp) started as a plot device around a fictional oak dining table in Christiansburg, Virginia—inside Michael P. Ragsdale's novel *The Eleventh Child: The Story of Jordan*.

Long before Antigravity made it possible to architect full multi-target compilers and Help Studio documentation portals locally in Y:\desktop-apps\, Michael was co-developing the world of The Eleventh Child with Gemini on the Web. What began as an authentic, character-driven capstone idea for an autistic Computer Science and Finance double major turned out to have such a clean mathematical core—applying linear resource types to Luca Pacioli's 1494 double-entry accounting identity—that RaggieSoft Labs promoted Fin# into a real MIT-licensed language specification and .NET compiler project!

August 26, 2015: "Question The Rules" on Geneva Street

In Part 3: Question The Rules: The Birth of Fin# (books/aubrie/e007/c001/p003.md), sophomore double-major Zoë Wren Brooks sits at the Geneva Street dining table enrolled simultaneously in two courses at Commonwealth Polytechnic Institute (CPI):

  1. CS 3214 — Compiler Design & Programming Languages
  2. ACIS 3115 — Intermediate Financial Accounting & Corporate Governance

Alongside her father Daniel Brooks, CPA, Zoë reviews a forensic accounting case study on the fictional December 2001 collapse of Houston-based Vectron Energy Corp., whose late-1990s television commercials famously dared viewers to "Question The Rules" while executives hid debt off balance sheet—forcing the passage of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX).

Sitting beside Zoë in his S.S. Freedom power wheelchair with his Engineering Thermodynamics textbook open, Jordan Fuller types the insight that sparks the entire language on his AAC device:

"In physics, you can question the rules of who owns the power lines, but you cannot question the First Law of Thermodynamics! Energy in a closed system cannot be created out of nothing or destroyed—it can only be transferred from one place to another."

"Because rules made by monopolies can be questioned. The laws of conservation cannot."

Zoë immediately connects Jordan's thermodynamics law to quantum no-cloning linear types and double-entry bookkeeping: if a programming language treats Capital<USD> as a conserved linear state rather than an overwriteable scalar number, an unbalanced off-balance-sheet entry cannot even compile (FIN0001 / FIN0002).
